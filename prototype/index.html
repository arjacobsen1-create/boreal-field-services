The Knowledge Graph — two worked examples from the design sheets

A connected view of key business objects and their relationships, built on governed data from Business Data Cloud (BDC). It gives agents the context to understand impact — not just isolated data — so they can investigate, recommend and assess consequences, for example a part shortage on a critical customer.

These are two separate worked examples from the design sheets — two datasets, not one graph. They sit apart from the January run’s stock picture on Pages 1 and 3, which keeps its own Home / Station B / Station C data.

How agents use it — with Example 2 (WO-3187)

  1. Stock agent sees a part shortage for WO-3187.
  2. Uses the graph to understand: which equipment is affected, the customer and SLA impact, alternative inventory locations, related open work orders.
  3. Recommends options (e.g. transfer from Station A, expedite, reschedule) with impact and approval needs.

KG governance & controls

  • Built from governed data (BDC).
  • Controlled data model — approved entities & relationships.
  • Data quality checks — accuracy, completeness, freshness.
  • Change management & lineage — who changed what, when.
  • Access control and audit trail — who can view and use the graph.

From Event to Outcome — the fuller 8-step flow Page 1 now runs

  1. Engineer records a part shortage on the tablet (offline). Stored locally (encrypted) + queued for sync. Arc: Capture.
  2. App validates and syncs when in coverage. CAP services check data, resolve conflicts.
  3. Integration updates S/4HANA. Work order / inventory is updated.
  4. Stock agent investigates using BDC + Knowledge Graph. Finds related parts, suppliers, impact. Arc: Reason.
  5. Agent proposes options (transfer, expedite, reschedule). Shows facts + uncertainty — no execution.
  6. Human approves the action. Via workflow / approval controls. Arc: Decide.
  7. Execution updates S/4HANA. Transaction goes through the business process. Arc: Execute.
  8. Outcome: issue resolved, customer informed, audit trail. Full traceability and monitoring — and the outcome feeds the Learn stage, improving agents and knowledge. Arc: Learn.

Key principle, from the fuller map: agents investigate and recommend — they don’t execute consequential actions.

Key Principles — from the map, enforced by the gate

  • The agent cannot turn stale data into confirmed stock.
  • The agent cannot turn an uncertain recovery plan into a customer commitment.
  • Agents use governed data (BDC) with business context (Knowledge Graph).
  • Application executes transactions; agents investigate and recommend.
  • Human approval for consequential actions.
  • The Knowledge Graph does not replace S/4HANA — it adds context and relationships so agents can reason across data.

Learn — the fifth stage of the arc, and what this page is for

Outcomes, data and feedback improve agents and knowledge. This evidence page is where Learn starts: the audit trail above, the corrected phantom stock, and the logged gate refusal are exactly the feedback the next run learns from — a station that keeps going stale gets watched harder, a part that keeps failing gets flagged earlier, and the graph’s relationships get corrected where reality disagreed with them.

Business goal, from the arc view: keep field work running (offline capable), use AI to investigate and recommend, keep S/4HANA as the system of record, and operate securely, with control and governance. Key value: trusted, governed business context (BDC + KG), accurate auditable transactions in S/4HANA, and continuous learning and improvement.
